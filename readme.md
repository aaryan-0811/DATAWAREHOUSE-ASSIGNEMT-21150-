Autonomous Driving Research: A Case Study and Analysis of the Udacity Self-Driving Car Dataset

This repository contains a detailed report analyzing the popular Udacity "Self-Driving Cars" dataset, a widely used resource for training object detection models in the autonomous driving space. The project's goal is to provide a comprehensive evaluation that goes beyond a surface-level description, performing a critical assessment of the dataset's structure, content, and real-world viability.

The report begins by contextualizing the dataset within the core principles of autonomous perception, including computer vision, object detection, and scene understanding. It then performs a deep dive into the dataset itself, detailing its attributes and structure. The most critical contribution of this analysis is the exposure of significant data integrity issues. Our findings reveal that a substantial portion of the dataset suffers from severe labeling errors, including thousands of unlabeled vehicles and hundreds of unlabeled pedestrians.

Ultimately, the report concludes that while the dataset is a valuable academic tool for an introduction to object detection, its data quality deficiencies make it unsuitable for training robust, safety-critical systems for production environments. This work underscores the paramount importance of data quality over quantity in machine learning for autonomous navigation and serves as a case study for researchers, students, and engineers on the challenges of creating reliable ground-truth data.
